# `CALATR` ($0087) — the address of a sprite attribute entry

> **Spec:** DRAFT 2 (2026-11-18), after one test-writer review, measured again (run 2). Measured on the VG-8020, alone and with a disk
> interface.

## Summary

`CALATR` returns in HL the VRAM address of sprite A's attribute entry: ATRBAS ($F928) plus 4 x A,
whatever the sprite size. DE holds the offset; A, BC, IX, IY and the interrupt state are kept.

Its F is what the addition leaves (H, C and bits 5 / 3), with S, Z and P/V as on entry.

zerobas does all of it the same.

## The specification

References: the Philips VG-8020's main BIOS, alone (row `N`) and with an NMS 1200 disk interface
(row `D`). Ours: the C-BIOS repack, diskless on row `N`, the disk build on `D`.

**Evidence.** **[XP rNN]**: case `NN` of the XSPR probe, run 2, on row `r`, both rows in
[`scratchpad/XSPR_run2.out`](../../scratchpad/XSPR_run2.out) (the two rows recorded the same bytes).
`probes/bios/spec/xspr.asm` documents the case table and the 20-byte record:
- Per case the probe sets RG1SAV ($F3E0), PATBAS ($F926) and ATRBAS ($F928) by hand, then BC $1357,
  DE $9BDF, HL $2468, IX $4321 and IY $8765 (all complemented in odd cases), the case's A, entry F
  and interrupt state, and calls. In cases 00-33 entry F is $00 in even cases and $FF in odd ones;
  cases 34-49 pair them the other way round and add $80, $40, $04, $A5 and $5A.
- It records every register, the interrupt state, RG1SAV, PATBAS and ATRBAS after the call. It does
  not look at the VDP or at other RAM.
- CALPAT: cases 00-17 and 34-39 -- A 0, 1, 7, $1F, $20, $7F, $FF with RG1SAV $E0 and $E2; A 1, 2, 3,
  5 with PATBAS $FFF8, $1234, $FFF0 and RG1SAV $E0-$E3; A 3, 5, 9 with RG1SAV $00, $02, $FD, $FF,
  $E1, $E3. CALATR: 18-25 and 40-43 -- A 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, $1F, $20, $40, $80, $FF; ATRBAS $1B00, $FFFE,
  $ABCD, $1234, $0F00 (a sum with the half-carry and no carry), $3F00. GSPSIZ: 26-33 and 44-49 --
  RG1SAV $E0-$E3, $00, $FF, $FD, $02, $80, $20, $40, $A2; A $A7, $00, $FF, $5A, $A5.
